# Prism blueprint (the AfterGlow 3D app)

Prism is the planned sixth app inside AfterGlow: open-source 3D tools for
scenes, models, materials, lighting, animation and rendering. You bring in a
model (or start from a primitive or extruded text and logo art), arrange it in
a scene, give it materials, light it, frame it with a camera, key it on a
timeline, and render stills or frame sequences that land in Halo, Flare and
Design. This blueprint is the gate before any code: it fixes the scope from
usage research, the components, every tier-1 journey, the use-case contracts,
the look, the quality bar and the build order. Feature packets
(`docs/blueprints/3d/features/`) come after the captain approves it.

Nothing exists for this app yet: no target, no Model, no engine, no issue.
Sources read on 2026-10-15: creative-apps `origin/dev` f2ef824 (`CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/design.md`, `docs/priorities.md`, `docs/engineering.md`,
`docs/c4/model.json`, `docs/factory/lead.md`, `docs/review-rubric.md`,
`docs/blueprints/app-audit.md`, `docs/blueprints/suite.md`, and the Flare and
Halo blueprints as the worked examples of shape and depth). The captain's look
decisions of 2026-10-15 apply: every app uses the `SuiteWindow` shell (glass
toolbar, sidebar, hideable inspector), Flare's floating glass tool palette,
and the app switcher row at the top of the window.

Files: this document; `docs/blueprints/3d/research.md` (raw research notes,
part 1); `docs/blueprints/3d/c4-planned.json` (part 4 as data);
`docs/blueprints/3d/contracts/` (part 6 as Swift that compiles on Linux, with
`check.sh`); `docs/blueprints/3d/waves.py` (part 9 as data, with its overlap
and C4 check).

## 0. Working name

The suite's names are short, one word, and either a light word (Flare, Halo)
or a plain noun for the job (Design, Photo, Library). Three proposals; the
captain picks. This document uses the first throughout, and every id
(`PrismModel`, `prism.*` identifiers, `.prism` files, `line:prism`) is a
mechanical rename if another is picked.

| # | Name | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prism** | A light word like Flare and Halo, and the one everyday 3D solid that is about light: it reads as "3D and lighting" without saying "3D". |
| 2 | **Depth** | A plain noun for what the app adds to the suite, the third dimension, in the register of Design and Photo; short in a menu ("Open in Depth" reads less well, so it is second). |
| 3 | **Form** | The sculptor's word for shape in space; one syllable like Photo's two, and it says modeling (tier 1 here, part 1.3) without promising a full modeler. |

Clean-room, as `docs/priorities.md` sets it: public sources only (open survey
data, public Q&A and forum counts, asset and add-on download counts, public
feature boards and issue trackers, help-center article lists). No 3D product
was installed, opened or run. Product names are replaced by the source key in

contain a name. Gathered 2026-10-15.

**The strongest source** is the raw data of the most-used open-source 3D
suite's 2025 user survey (5,102 responses, CC BY 4.0), which asks "what do

### 1.2 What the open-source community is asking for now

- **Approachability for designers.** The biggest developer-forum threads are
  about the interface (4,020 posts on paper cuts; 1,409 on interface design),
  and an open letter (314 posts) says the suite "is rather complicated and has
  a steep learning curve" for designers who want to add 3D. People now build
  AI helpers just to point beginners at the right button
  (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45945890).
- **Interchange that works.** USD and USDZ support is the top format request
  on the most-starred web 3D library (73,
  https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/14219) and an open game engine
  (36, https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/7744); USDZ
  exports from the open-source suite fail in Apple's AR tools (missing
  textures, one animation only, https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/734220);
  the glTF exporter's most-commented issues are multiple animations (37) and
  speed (33).
- **Drag-and-drop import** of standard formats (210 + 136 votes) and **SVG
  to 3D** (designer-side forum, about 60 likes per request).
- **Direct, precise modeling**: base-point snapping (555), push/pull (510),
  align and distribute (153), editing the origin or pivot (71).
- **Real grouping** (364) and **undo everywhere** (a mesh-processing tool's
  top open issues are glTF 2.0 support and undo,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/441,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/376).
- **Fast on Apple silicon**, and a Mac-native feel (R18).
- **Texturing tools and animation/rigging tools** are the two most-wanted
  projects in the survey (42.2%, 33.3%; the rigging half is a non-goal here); motion-graphics users say the open-source suite
  "takes many more clicks" than the commercial motion tool they know.

### 1.3 What the research decides

1. **The hypothesis holds for the loop and changes for modeling.** Import,
   arrange, materials, lights, cameras, keys and render are all in the top
   ten (R2, R4 to R6, R8, R10). But **basic polygon modeling is the single
   most-done task** (R1: 77.5% overall, 85% of motion-graphics people, 84.4%
   of graphic designers), the top all-time question tag and the
   second-largest forum category (after interface basics, R3). So modeling
   is **tier 1 and in v1**, not tier 2 or 3: primitives plus an edit mode with
   extrude, inset, bevel, loop cut, merge, delete, fill and smooth shading.
   The subdivision and mirror modifiers that make box modeling usable are
   tier 2 and also in v1 (R7). Sculpting, procedural nodes, rigging and
   simulation stay out (R13 to R16).
2. **Render is tier 1, not tier 2** as it is for Halo and Flare: a 3D scene
   has no other way to reach the motion and design apps, and rendering is the
   third-largest question topic (R4).
3. **Interface basics are tier 1 work, not polish** (R3): the largest forum
   category and the largest idea category are about finding your way. That
   sets the look (part 7) and the first-use clarity bar (part 8).
4. **Environment light (an HDRI) ships with the first lighting feature** (R6:
   60% of free asset downloads).
5. **Text and vector-art extrusion is tier 2 in v1** (R9): it is the most
   common way designers start a 3D piece from Design and the suite's own
   hand-off in.
6. **FBX** is the most-asked format (820 titles) but it is a closed format;
   opening it is the captain's legal call (CLAUDE.md, Clean-room). v1 reads
   glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY and USDZ; part 10 asks.

## 2. Brief

### 2.1 The problem

Motion designers and graphic designers increasingly need 3D: a logo that
turns, a product shot, a title with depth, a scene behind a lower third. The
free tools that do this are built for generalist 3D artists. Their
interfaces are the most-discussed problem in their own communities (R3,
1.2), their Mac performance is a long-running thread (R18), and getting a
render into a compositor or a layout tool means exporting files and
importing them by hand. Prism is a Mac-native, free, open 3D app inside
AfterGlow that does the everyday 3D loop well: model or bring in an object,
place it, give it a material, light it, frame it with a camera, animate it,
and render it straight into Halo, Flare or Design.

### 2.2 Who uses it

| Person | Share of the research sample | Every session | Weekly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motion designer adding 3D** (Halo's and Flare's users) | 25.5% do motion graphics; of them 94% animate, 85% model, 67% light (R1, R2, R6) | Open or make a scene, model or import an object, key a move or turn, render a sequence to Halo | Extruded titles and logos, camera moves, environment light |
| **Graphic or product designer** (Design's and Photo's users) | 39.8% do graphic design, 32.6% product visualization; designers model (81%) and light (51%) (R1) | Model a simple product or prop, apply materials, light it, render a still to Design | Import a model from a marketplace (glTF, USDZ), export USDZ for AR |
| **Hobby generalist** learning 3D on a Mac | 52.9% use their tool daily; 50.3% earn nothing from it | Model, material, light, render | Animate, export to share |

### 2.3 Non-goals

- Sculpting with brushes, procedural node graphs, rigging and skinned
  characters, cloth, fluid, particle and rigid-body simulation (R13 to R16):
  each is a tool of its own.
- Compositing and video editing: Halo does that with Prism's renders.
- A production path tracer in v1. The v1 renderer is the viewport's own
  engine offscreen (part 4); a path tracer is a later tier-3 item.
- Game-engine features: physics at run time, scripting, level design.
- CAD: exact solids, dimensions and tolerances, technical drawings.
- UV unwrapping and texture painting in v1 (R12, R5): primitives come with
  UVs and imported models keep theirs.
- Reading or writing closed formats without the captain's sign-off (FBX, any
  vendor's native scene file); plugins; cloud or collaboration; iPad.

### 2.4 The v1 line

| Area | In v1 | Out of v1 (later, in order) |

**The line in one sentence:** a designer opens Prism, makes or imports an
object, shapes it with a few modeling tools, gives it a material and light,
keys a turn, and sends the render to Halo, without leaving AfterGlow.

## 3. Feature catalog

Every feature, tiered from part 1 by the `docs/priorities.md` rule (tier 1:
nearly every session; tier 2: weekly; tier 3: specialist). Each entry says
what the person does, what they see, its empty, error and loading states,
its keys, and how it connects to the other AfterGlow apps. "Viewport keys"
act only while the viewport has focus (as Halo's Space and I/O act only in
its viewer and timeline), so typing in a field never moves anything; the
menu item shows the key in its help, not as a key equivalent. Every control
works or is disabled with its reason (`unavailableHelp`).

### Tier 1 (v1, the everyday loop)

**P-F1 New scene and scene settings** (R2, R4; the journey rule). File > New
Scene (⌘N) or the AfterGlow home's "New Scene" tile shows the New Scene sheet
(presets, size, rate, length; part 7.2), and Create opens a window with a
starter scene: a ground grid, a 1-unit cube at the origin, one directional
light, the environment at a neutral grey, and a camera framing the cube at
1920 × 1080, 30 fps, 3 s. With no object selected, the inspector shows the
SCENE section: width, height, frame rate (24, 25, 30, 60), length in seconds
and frames, environment (part P-F13). *Empty:* File > New Empty Scene
(⌥⌘N) makes a scene with nothing but the grid; the viewport shows the
caption "Empty scene. Add a shape from the palette, or drop a model here."
with Add Cube as its one prominent button. *Error:* a typed size outside
16–8192 or a length of 0 keeps Create disabled (in the sheet) or stays
unapplied (in SCENE), with `PrismSceneSettings.problems`' sentence under the
field ("Width must be a whole number from 16 to 8192."). *Loading:* none. *Keys:* ⌘N, ⌥⌘N.
*Connects:* the home's New tile and the switcher row's Prism item create
through the suite's `DocumentCreating` (suite.md UC3); presets "Square
1080", "Vertical 9:16" and "Landscape 1080p" match Halo's composition sizes
so a render drops into a Halo composition of the same size.

**P-F2 Open, save, Open Recent** (journey rule; R8's "drag and drop" asks).
File > Open… (⌘O), Save (⌘S), Save As… (⇧⌘S) and Open Recent. A scene is a
`.prism` package: `scene.json` plus one binary file per mesh and texture
(part 6, `PrismFilePackage`), so a 2-million-triangle scene opens without
parsing a giant JSON. The title shows the file name and an unsaved dot.
*Empty:* a new scene is "Untitled Scene" until saved. *Error:* a file that
isn't a scene, a newer format, a damaged package (a mesh that points at a
vertex it doesn't have, a missing buffer, two objects with one id, a parent
cycle) or one over 512 MB is refused with `PrismFileError.message` in an
alert, and the open window is untouched; a save to a read-only folder shows
"Not saved" in the title and an alert with the reason. *Loading:* opening
shows a progress row in the viewport within 100 ms (`beginOpen`), decodes
off the main actor, and lands with `finishOpen`. *Keys:* standard. *Connects:*
every open and save is recorded in the suite's one recents list, so the home
and the switcher's recents show it with a thumbnail (the last viewport
frame); Finder double-click on a `.prism` opens Prism through the suite
router (suite.md 2.6).

**P-F3 Undo and redo** (journey rule). Edit > Undo ⟨step⟩ (⌘Z) and Redo
(⇧⌘Z) for every scene and mesh edit, 100 steps, each named ("Undo Move",
"Undo Extrude", "Undo Change Material"). A gizmo drag, an inspector scrub
and a modeling-tool drag are each one step; selection, navigation, the
playhead and playback are never steps. *Empty:* Undo disabled with "Nothing
to undo." *Error:* none (an undo can't fail). *Loading:* none; PB-8 bounds
it. *Keys:* ⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z. *Connects:* a hand-off out or in (P-F18, P-F25) is one
step in the app that receives it, never in the sender.

**P-F4 Viewport navigation** (R3: the largest question and idea category).
Orbit with a two-finger trackpad scroll or Option-drag, pan with Shift added
or Space held while dragging (the suite's canvas rule), dolly with pinch or
⌘-scroll. The
viewport shows the scene shaded by its lights and environment, a ground grid
with the X axis red-tinted and Z blue-tinted, and an axis widget in the
top-right corner whose six ends click to the front, back, left, right, top
and bottom views (orthographic) and whose center returns to perspective. A
status pill row under the viewport reads the view ("Perspective", "Top ·
Orthographic", "Camera: Camera 1"), the triangle count and the frame.
*Empty:* the empty-scene caption of P-F1. *Error:* none: navigation clamps
(distance 0.01 to 100,000 units, pitch ±89.9°). *Loading:* a model still
importing shows its progress row over the viewport; navigation stays live.
*Keys (viewport):* F frame selected, A frame all (with nothing selected F
does the same), ⌥⌘1 front, ⌥⌘3 right, ⌥⌘7 top, ⌥⌘5 toggle orthographic,
⌥⌘0 look through the active camera; ⌥-arrow keys orbit 15° (⇧⌥: pan); plain
← and → step a frame (P-F15).
*Connects:* none out; the axis widget and the view pills are Prism's own
layout of SuiteUI parts (part 7).

**P-F5 Select** (R3, R11). Click an object in the viewport or its row in
the outliner to select it; Shift-click adds or removes; drag on empty space
draws a selection box; Select All (⌘A), Deselect All (⇧⌘A), Invert. The
selected object is drawn with an accent outline and its gizmo; the outliner
row is highlighted; the inspector shows its sections. *Empty:* clicking
empty space deselects and the inspector returns to SCENE. *Error:* none.
*Loading:* none. *Keys:* ⌘A, ⇧⌘A; Escape deselects in the viewport. 
*Connects:* none.

**P-F6 Outliner: organize the scene** (R11: a real grouping object, 364
votes). The sidebar's SCENE section lists every object as a tree with its
kind's SF Symbol (cube, lightbulb, video, folder). Rename by double-click,
drag to reorder or to drop into a group, the eye toggles visibility, Object >
Group (⌘G) wraps the selection in a group at their center, Ungroup (⇧⌘G),
Duplicate (⌘D, with its children), Delete (⌫ in the outliner or viewport).
Parenting keeps the object where it is on screen. *Empty:* "No objects yet.
Add one from the palette." *Error:* dropping a group into its own child is

or its children." shows under the outliner; an empty name keeps the old one
with "Enter a name." *Loading:* an import adds its rows when it lands.
*Keys:* ⌘G, ⇧⌘G, ⌘D, ⌫, H hide, ⌥H show all (viewport and outliner focus).
*Connects:* none.

**P-F7 Add primitives, groups, lights and cameras** (R1: the extra-primitives
add-on's 1M downloads). The palette's Add tool (and Object > Add ▸) offers
Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Plane, Torus, Empty Group, the three v1 lights
and Camera. The new object lands at the center of the view on the ground (or at
the origin when the view looks below the horizon), selected, with the Move
gizmo on. *Empty:* not applicable. *Error:* none. *Loading:* none. *Keys:*
⇧⌘A is taken by Deselect All, so Add has no single shortcut; the palette's
Add tool is O ("object") with the canvas focused, which opens the menu.
*Connects:* none.

**P-F8 Transform: gizmo, inspector and snapping** (R1, R11). Move (W),
Rotate (E) and Scale (R) tools show a gizmo on the selection: three axis
arrows and three plane squares for move, three rings and a view ring for
rotate, three axis handles and a center cube for scale. Dragging a handle
moves live (a preview, not yet a step) and mouse-up commits one step. The
inspector's TRANSFORM rows show Position, Rotation and Scale X, Y, Z as
Studio bars (drag, click to type, arrows step, Shift ×10, Option ×0.1).
Holding ⌘ while dragging snaps (0.25 units, 15°, 0.1×); View > Snap to Grid
turns it on for every drag. *Empty:* with nothing selected the Move, Rotate
and Scale tools are enabled but show no gizmo, and the canvas hint reads
"Select an object to move it." *Error:* a typed "abc" keeps the value and
shows "Position X needs a number." under the row; a scale of 0 is kept at

Escape during a drag cancels it. *Connects:* none.

**P-F9 Import models** (R8). File > Import… (⇧⌘I) or drop files on the

*Error:* an unsupported extension names the formats Prism reads; a glTF or
OBJ whose buffers or MTL are missing names the missing file ("scene.bin is
missing. Put it next to the model and import again."); a damaged file says
what is wrong; over 5,000,000 triangles is refused before anything is added.
Skipped parts (skins, morph targets, extra UV sets, cameras in the file) are
listed in one dismissible note under the viewport, never an alert.
*Loading:* a progress row with the file name and Cancel within 100 ms; the
parse runs off the main actor; the viewport stays live. *Keys:* ⇧⌘I.
*Connects:* a model dragged from a suite asset (suite.md assets, tier 2
there) imports the same way.

**P-F10 Edit mode and element selection** (R1: edit-mode 2,946, vertices
2,489). Double-click a mesh or press Tab (viewport) to enter edit mode on it;

triangles; the selection's center X, Y, Z as bars). 1, 2 and 3 switch
between vertex, edge and face selection; click, Shift-click, box drag, ⌘A,
and Option-click on an edge selects its loop. Tab or Escape leaves edit
mode. *Empty:* "Select vertices, edges or faces to edit them." as the canvas
hint while nothing is selected. *Error:* edit mode on a light, camera or
group is disabled with "Only objects with a shape can be edited." *Loading:*
none. *Keys (viewport):* Tab, 1, 2, 3, ⌘A, ⌥-click. *Connects:* none.

**P-F11 Modeling tools** (R1, R11's push/pull). In edit mode the palette
holds Select (V), Move (W), Rotate (E), Scale (R), Extrude (X), Inset (I),
Bevel (B) and Loop Cut (L), and Mesh menu items Merge at Center (M), Fill
(⇧F), Delete (⌫), Flip Normals, Shade Smooth and Shade Flat. Extrude, Inset
and Bevel act by dragging in the viewport (the distance follows the pointer,

Loop Cut previews the yellow-marked loop under the pointer and a click cuts
it (scroll before clicking sets 1 to 64 cuts). Each tool's result is one
named step and keeps the new elements selected. *Empty:* a tool with nothing
selected shows its hint ("Select faces to extrude.") and does nothing; the
workspace shows the hint and never calls `apply`, so `.nothingSelected` is
only a guard.
*Error:* a refused operation (extrude in vertex mode, merge of one vertex)
shows the reason under the viewport's status pills; a result that would
damage the mesh is refused whole. *Loading:* none; PB-6 bounds each step on
a 24,576-face mesh. *Keys:* as listed, viewport focus only. *Connects:*
none.

**P-F12 Materials** (R5). Each scene keeps a material list (the sidebar's

*Connects:* drop or paste an image from Photo or Library as Base Color

it lands. *Keys:* none. *Connects:* an environment image can come from Photo
or Library by drop or paste (P-F25).

**P-F14 Cameras** (R10). Add Camera, or View > New Camera from View (⌥⌘C)

**P-F15 Keyframes, timeline and playback** (R2). Every keyable row in the

*Empty:* the timeline reads "Nothing animated yet. Click a stopwatch in the

End, K (viewport and timeline focus). *Connects:* the timeline is the same SuiteUI `TimelineStrip`

**P-F16 Easing presets** (R2; the same evidence as Flare and Halo). Pick a

### 4.1 Platform: what draws, renders, imports and exports

Checked in Apple's documentation on 2026-10-15 (raw notes in
`research.md` section F):

| Need | Pick | Why (Apple docs) | Not picked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interactive viewport | **RealityKit** in a `RealityView` | `RealityView` is available on macOS 15 and later and renders non-AR content on macOS (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityview, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewcameracontent). It has every v1 light (directional, point and spot with shadows: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/directionallightcomponent, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/spotlightcomponent), image-based light (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/imagebasedlightcomponent), both cameras (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/perspectivecameracomponent, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/orthographiccameracomponent), a PBR material covering base color, metallic, roughness and emissive (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/physicallybasedmaterial), and editable meshes updated from the CPU or GPU (`LowLevelMesh`, macOS 15: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/lowlevelmesh) | **SceneKit**: "SceneKit is deprecated, use RealityKit instead" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/scenekit; WWDC25 session 288 calls it maintenance mode). **Own Metal renderer for v1**: months of work for what RealityKit gives; kept for the tier-3 path tracer |
| Final render | **RealityKit's `RealityRenderer` offscreen** into an `MTLTexture`, the same engine and scene as the viewport, so what you see is what renders | `RealityRenderer` "displays a RealityKit scene in an existing Metal workflow"; its `CameraOutput` takes color textures (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityrenderer, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityrenderer/cameraoutput) | A path tracer on Metal ray tracing (`MTLAccelerationStructure`, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlaccelerationstructure, with the macOS 26 denoising scaler https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metalfx/mtlfxtemporaldenoisedscaler): tier 3, P-F32 |
| Movies | **AVFoundation** `AVAssetWriter` with `inputPixelBufferReceiver(for:pixelBufferAttributes:)` (macOS 26; the older pixel-buffer adaptor is deprecated in macOS 27); H.264 and ProRes 4444 | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetwriter, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetwriter/inputpixelbufferreceiver(for:pixelbufferattributes:), https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avvideocodectype/prores4444. Whether ProRes 4444 keeps alpha is *unverified* in Apple's page; the render feature's first task proves it with a decoded frame (PF-10) and falls back to HEVC with alpha (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avvideocodectype/hevcwithalpha) | – |
| Import USD, USDA, USDC, USDZ | **Model I/O** `MDLAsset` on macOS, converted to Prism meshes and materials in `PrismModelIOImporter` | Model I/O imports `.usd`, `.usda`, `.usdc`, `.usdz`, `.obj`, `.ply`, `.stl`, `.abc` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/modelio/mdlasset/canimportfileextension(_:)) | RealityKit's `Entity(contentsOf:)` gives entities, not editable data we own |
| Import glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY | **creative-core, pure Swift** (C3D-2) | Nothing Apple ships reads glTF (no glTF API in Model I/O, RealityKit or USDKit; research F). Pure Swift is Linux-tested and the format is an open Khronos spec | Model I/O for OBJ, STL, PLY: possible, but then the Linux tests could not cover them |
| Export GLB, OBJ, STL, USDZ | **creative-core, pure Swift** (C3D-2): USDZ written as USDA text in an uncompressed zip with 64-byte aligned files | RealityKit's `write(to:)` writes `.reality`, not USD (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/entity/write(to:)); USDKit, Apple's USD API, needs macOS 27 (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usdkit); Model I/O documents export of `.obj` and `.stl` only (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/modelio/mdlasset/canexportfileextension(_:)) | USDKit behind an adapter once the suite can require macOS 27 (part 10, Q5) |
| Images (textures, HDRIs) | **ImageIO** in `PrismImageDecoder` (macOS); the fake decoder in Linux tests | Platform standard; Photo already decodes PNG, JPEG and HEIC through ImageIO (suite.md 0.1) | – |
| Glyph outlines | **Core Text** in `PrismGlyphOutliner` | The system's font outlines; Halo's text layers use Core Text the same way | – |

**What runs where.** The Model owns the whole document and evaluates it:
keys, world matrices, modifiers and the draw list are pure Swift, tested on
Linux. RealityKit only draws what the draw list says: `PrismRealityBridge`
keeps one entity per draw item, updates its transform, mesh and material
when the list changes, and never animates on its own (RealityKit's
animation types are not used: there is no keyframe type with Bézier easing
in it, research F). The edit-mode dots and lines, the selection outline and
the gizmo are not RealityKit content: the Model projects them to 2D
(`PrismOverlay`), and a SwiftUI `Canvas` over the `RealityView` draws them,
so picking a handle is Model code too.

### 4.5 Rules the components keep

- **One state value.** `PrismWorkspace` is the single `Sendable`,
  `Equatable` value behind the window. W0 declares every stored property v1
  needs in `PrismWorkspace.swift` (document, services, history, playback,
  viewpoint, edit mode and element selection, the gizmo or tool drag in
  progress, file state, import job, render job, refusal), each written only
  by its own feature's extension file, so no later feature edits it.
- **Undo** is whole-document snapshots, 100 steps (as Halo's D2). Meshes
  are arrays inside value types, so a step that doesn't touch a mesh shares
  its storage; a modeling step copies only the mesh it edits. PB-9 bounds
  the cost on `crowd` and PB-9b the open of `huge`. A command engine in creative-core is a
  later item (10, D3).
- **The draw list is the one truth.** Viewport, render and export read the
  scene through `FrameDrawing` and `ModifierEvaluating`; nothing in PrismApp
  evaluates keys or modifiers.
- **Live conformances that need Apple frameworks live in PrismApp**
  (`prism-platform`) and are installed into `.live` by `PrismLaunch`; the
  Model never imports them.
- **Suite adoption.** Prism adopts the suite like the five apps
  (suite.md 8.4): `.suiteApp(.prism, host:)` on its scene, its document type
  `org.creativesuite.prism.scene` (`.prism`, a package conforming to
  `com.apple.package` and `public.content`) and pasteboard type
  `org.creativesuite.prism.objects` in `SuiteTypeRegistry`, the switcher row
  item and ⌃⌘6. Each is a small task on the suite line or a protected file
  (`Package.swift`, `scripts/bundle-apps`): part 9.5.

## 5. Journeys

Every tier-1 feature has a journey: an XCUITest that launches the release
bundle, works the window like a person and shoots the whole window after
every step (`docs/engineering.md`, Human-like UI tests); the app audit runs
on every shot under a new `prism` key in `UITests/audit-baseline.json`,
starting at 0 for every check (adding an app at 0 is not a loosening,
app-audit.md).

**Files.** `UITests/PrismJourneyTests.swift` (W0) plus one
`UITests/PrismJourneyTests+<Area>.swift` per feature, each its own
`XCTestCase`, so no two features edit one journey file. The per-file journey
template (SuiteUI QH-4, #333) is closed, so features journey in parallel
from W1. W0 adds the `PrismJourneyTests` bundle to `project.yml`. Fixture
files live in `UITests/Fixtures/prism/`, each owned by one feature (part 9)
and each with its source and license in `UITests/Fixtures/prism/NOTICE.md`;
models are made by us or CC0 / CC BY 4.0 only.

**Modes.** A journey first runs on `-PrismFixture <name>` (fake services,
fixture scene) and is flipped to `-PrismSample <name>` (live services, same
scene) by the task that lands the live type. P-J25 runs live from its
first PR.

**Finding things.** By the title a person reads, else by identifier
(`prism.<area>.<name>`). The viewport can't be read through pixels in
XCUITest, so `prism.viewport` carries the Model's `viewportSummary` as its
accessibility value ("Frame 12 · Camera 1 · 4 objects · Selected: Bottle"),
built and tested in PrismModel. To click an object or a gizmo handle, the
journey reads the `prism.objects` JSON that `PrismControls` writes under
`-PrismControlsProbe` (each object's and each visible handle's projected
center in normalized viewport points, from `PrismOverlay`) and clicks there.
State changes are awaited with `waitForExistence` or a value predicate
(2 s); timing claims come only from `SuiteFrameProbe` marks (on dev),
never an XCUI query. XCUITest on macOS has no pinch, so dolly is driven by
⌘-scroll and pinch is covered by a Model test of `dolly(_:)`. Files reach the
app by launch argument (`-PrismImport`, `-PrismOpenFile`); the drop path is
covered by a Model test of the same import entry point.

**Fixtures** (`PrismFixtures`, built in code; the live sample of the same
name is the same scene through `.live`):

## 6. Contracts

One Swift protocol per use case, with exact signatures and a fake for each,
so a feature's views and journeys can be built on the fake while its logic
is built on the live type, and the two meet by swapping one file. They are
Model-target code: Foundation through the FoundationEssentials pattern, the
C math module for `sin` and `cos` (as `PhotoStraighten.swift` does), value
types, `Sendable`, Swift 6, typed throws. No SwiftUI, AppKit, RealityKit or
other Darwin-only API.

**Proof.** The files in `docs/blueprints/3d/contracts/` are the contracts
as W0 will copy them: `Values.swift` (part 6.1), `Rules.swift` (6.3),
`Contracts.swift` (6.2, printed below), `Fakes.swift` (6.4),
`Services.swift` (6.5) and `main.swift`, about 60 proof checks of the fakes
against the behavior this blueprint states (primitive counts, parenting
and cycles, keys and easing, picking, gizmo snapping, extrude, inset, loop
cut, merge and delete counts, text, SVG and image decode, mirror and
subdivision counts, material clamps, file round trip and the three
refusals, OBJ export, render pixels and size refusal, 48-frame sequence,
cancel and sink failure, hand-off requests and refusals, unbuilt help).
`docs/blueprints/3d/contracts/check.sh` compiles them with `swiftc
-swift-version 6 -warnings-as-errors` and runs them. Run on 2026-10-15 with
Swift 6.2 on Ubuntu 24.04: `contracts: PASS (6 primitives, 25 services)`.
A deliberately broken expectation makes it print `contracts: FAIL`, so the
checks bite.

**Rules every conformance keeps.**

- Reads take values; writes take the scene `inout` and throw a typed error
  (`PrismEditError`, `PrismImportError`, `PrismFileError`,
  `PrismRenderError`), changing nothing when they throw (fakes write to a
  copy). Each error's `message` is the sentence the UI shows, so journeys
  assert the same text the Model test does.
- No contract records undo. `PrismWorkspace.edit(_ name:, coalesceKey:,
  _ body:)` does (part 4.5), so fakes and live types are tested without a
  workspace.
- **No copies between fake and live.** Rules both need (sampling, world
  matrices, cycle checks, file damage, trimming) live once in
  `Live/PrismRules.swift`; when a live type lands, its fake calls it or
  becomes a thin wrapper. Fakes differ only where their header says so.
- The workspace checks for an empty selection and shows the tool's hint
  before calling a contract, so contract guards like `.nothingSelected`
  never reach the person.
- Every service has `unavailableHelp`; `MeshEditing` also answers per
  operation, so one tool can wait for the engine while the others work.
- One conformance suite per contract
  (`Tests/PrismModelTests/<Contract>ContractTests.swift`, W0) runs the same
  expectations against the fake and, once it exists, the live type
  (`@Test(arguments:)` over both). A live type is done when it passes its
  suite.

### 6.2 The protocols (`Contracts.swift`)

```swift
public protocol PrismService: Sendable {
    /// Nil when the service can act; else the sentence a disabled control shows.
    var unavailableHelp: String? { get }
}

// MARK: Bring things in

/// UC1 Import a model file (glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY in core; USD and USDZ through the macOS importer).
public protocol SceneImporting: PrismService {
    func canImport(_ format: PrismImportFormat) -> Bool
    /// Files the model points at (glTF buffers and images, an OBJ's .mtl), so the app can read
    /// them from the same folder before `importModel`.
    func sidecarNames(in data: Data, format: PrismImportFormat) throws(PrismImportError) -> [String]
    func importModel(_ data: Data, format: PrismImportFormat, name: String,
                     sidecars: [String: Data]) throws(PrismImportError) -> PrismImportResult
}

/// UC2 Add a primitive or an empty, a light or a camera.
public protocol PrimitiveMaking: PrismService {
    /// A mesh of about `detail` segments around (spheres, cylinders, cones, tori); 1 unit across.
    func mesh(for primitive: PrismPrimitive, id: PrismID, detail: Int) -> PrismMesh
    /// The default light of a kind, placed 4 units up and aimed at the origin.
    func light(_ kind: PrismLightKind) -> (PrismLight, PrismTransform)
}

/// UC3 Extrude text or vector art into a solid with depth and a bevel.
public protocol ShapeExtruding: PrismService {
    func extrude(_ outline: PrismOutline, _ extrusion: PrismExtrusion, id: PrismID,
                 name: String) throws(PrismEditError) -> PrismMesh
}

/// UC3b Read vector art (SVG 1.1 paths, basic shapes and solid fills; Design's pasteboard SVG) as shapes.
public protocol VectorArtReading: PrismService {
    /// Filled closed shapes in document order, y up, in SVG user units; skipped features as warnings.
    func shapes(fromSVG data: Data) throws(PrismImportError) -> (shapes: [PrismVectorShape], warnings: [String])
}

/// UC3c Decode an image (PNG, JPEG, HEIC) for a texture or the environment; the macOS live type uses ImageIO.
public protocol ImageDecoding: PrismService {
    func decode(_ data: Data, name: String, id: PrismID) throws(PrismImportError) -> PrismTexture
}

/// UC22 Evaluate a mesh's modifiers (subdivision, mirror) for the viewport, render and export.
public protocol ModifierEvaluating: PrismService {
    /// The mesh after `modifiers` in order; refuses a result over `PrismImportError.maximumTriangles`.
    func evaluate(_ mesh: PrismMesh, modifiers: [PrismModifier], forRender: Bool) throws(PrismEditError) -> PrismMesh
}

/// UC3 (platform half) Glyph outlines for a string; the macOS live type uses Core Text.
public protocol GlyphOutlining: PrismService {
    /// The string's outline at `size` units per em, baseline at y = 0, left edge at x = 0.
    func outline(of text: String, fontName: String, size: Double) -> PrismOutline
}

/// UC21 Model: edit a mesh's vertices, edges and faces (tier 1 by research R1, R3, R9).
public protocol MeshEditing: PrismService {
    /// Nil when this operation can run, else why not (a fake may leave some to the live engine).
    func unavailableHelp(for op: PrismMeshOpKind) -> String?
    /// The element under a ray in a mesh placed at `world`: the nearest face hit, or in vertex and
    /// edge mode the vertex or edge of that face nearest the hit point.
    func pick(_ ray: PrismRay, mesh: PrismMesh, world: Mat4, mode: PrismSelectMode) -> PrismMeshElement?
    /// Applies an operation to the selection; returns the new mesh and what is selected after it
    /// (the new faces after an extrude or inset, the new loop after a loop cut). Refuses with
    /// `.nothingSelected` on an empty selection, or `.outOfRange` with the reason.
    func apply(_ op: PrismMeshOp, to mesh: PrismMesh, selection: PrismMeshSelection,
               mode: PrismSelectMode) throws(PrismEditError) -> (mesh: PrismMesh, selection: PrismMeshSelection)
    /// The edge loop through an edge (Option-click selects it); the edge alone at a pole or boundary.
    func loop(through edge: PrismEdge, in mesh: PrismMesh) -> [PrismEdge]
}

// MARK: Arrange

/// UC4 Where things are: local and world transforms at a frame, parenting.
public protocol SceneArranging: PrismService {
    func worldMatrix(of node: PrismID, in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int) -> Mat4?
    /// World-space bounds of a node and its children at a frame; empty for lights, cameras and groups with nothing.
    func worldBounds(of nodes: [PrismID], in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int) -> Box3
    /// Moves `node` under `parent` (nil: the scene root) at the outliner position `index`.
    /// With `keepWorld` the node stays where it is on screen. A cycle is refused.
    func reparent(_ node: PrismID, to parent: PrismID?, index: Int, keepWorld: Bool,
                  in scene: inout PrismScene, at frame: Int) throws(PrismEditError)
}

/// UC5 Orbit, pan, dolly and frame the free view. Pure camera math.
public protocol ViewNavigating: PrismService {
    /// Drag deltas in points; 0.25° per point.
    func orbit(_ view: PrismViewCamera, dx: Double, dy: Double) -> PrismViewCamera
    /// Moves the target in the view plane by a fraction of the view's height.
    func pan(_ view: PrismViewCamera, dx: Double, dy: Double, viewHeightPoints: Double) -> PrismViewCamera
    /// Scroll or pinch: factor > 1 moves closer; clamped to the view camera's distance limits.
    func dolly(_ view: PrismViewCamera, factor: Double) -> PrismViewCamera
    /// Fits `bounds` in the view at `aspect` with a 10% margin; an empty box resets to the default view.
    func frame(_ bounds: Box3, in view: PrismViewCamera, aspect: Double) -> PrismViewCamera
    func preset(_ preset: PrismViewPreset, from view: PrismViewCamera) -> PrismViewCamera
    /// View matrix and eye for a viewpoint (the free view, or a scene camera at a frame).
    func viewMatrix(for viewpoint: PrismViewpoint, in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int) -> (view: Mat4, eye: Vec3)?
}

/// UC6 Click to select: the ray under the pointer and what it hits first.
public protocol ViewportPicking: PrismService {
    func ray(at point: ViewPoint, viewpoint: PrismViewpoint, in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int,
             aspect: Double) -> PrismRay?
    /// The nearest visible mesh node the ray hits, by triangle; nil for empty space.
    func hit(_ ray: PrismRay, in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int) -> PrismHit?
}

/// UC7 Move, rotate and scale with the on-canvas gizmo.
public protocol GizmoDragging: PrismService {
    /// Starts a drag on a handle of the selected node's gizmo; nil if the node is gone.
    func begin(_ mode: PrismGizmoMode, handle: PrismGizmoHandle, node: PrismID, at point: ViewPoint,
               viewpoint: PrismViewpoint, in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int, aspect: Double) -> PrismGizmoDrag?
    /// The node's new local transform for the pointer at `point`. Never writes the scene.
    func transform(for drag: PrismGizmoDrag, to point: ViewPoint, viewpoint: PrismViewpoint,
                   in scene: PrismScene, at frame: Int, aspect: Double, snap: PrismSnap) -> PrismTransform
}

// MARK: Look

/// UC8 Give an object a material and edit it.
public protocol MaterialEditing: PrismService {
    /// The starting looks offered in the material picker: Matte, Plastic, Rubber, Metal, Chrome, Gold, Glow.
    var presets: [PrismMaterial] { get }

    func undo(_ current: PrismDocumentState, history: inout PrismHistory) -> PrismDocumentState?
    func redo(_ current: PrismDocumentState, history: inout PrismHistory) -> PrismDocumentState?

/// The undo stack the workspace stores. 100 steps.
public struct PrismHistory: Sendable, Hashable {
    public struct Step: Sendable, Hashable {
        public var name: String, coalesceKey: String?, state: PrismDocumentState
        public init(name: String, coalesceKey: String?, state: PrismDocumentState) {
            self.name = name; self.coalesceKey = coalesceKey; self.state = state
        }
    }
    public var undo: [Step], redo: [Step], coalescing: Bool
    public init(undo: [Step] = [], redo: [Step] = [], coalescing: Bool = false) {
        self.undo = undo; self.redo = redo; self.coalescing = coalescing
    }
    public static let limit = 100
    public var undoTitle: String? { undo.last.map { "Undo \($0.name)" } }
    public var redoTitle: String? { redo.last.map { "Redo \($0.name)" } }
}
```

### 6.3 Shared rules (`Rules.swift`)

`PrismRules`: `sample(_:base:at:)` (the earlier key's easing between keys,
the end values outside them), `base` and `setBase` (with the clamps: scale

decoded document can be damaged, as one sentence), `trimmed` and `after`
(FoundationEssentials has no trimming or `range(of:)`). W0 maps `sample`
onto FlareCore's `Timeline.value(of:at:)` and `PrismEasing` onto FlareCore
`Easing`.

## 8. Quality bar

Every item names where it is measured and the test that measures it.
"Gate" means a test fails over the number; "report-only" means the test
records the number and fails only on too few samples, until ten green CI
runs set the gate at the measured CI p95 × 1.5, never tighter than the
device target (`docs/review-rubric.md`, How gates are set). A gate never
loosens without the captain.

### 8.1 Input-to-screen speed budgets

Two halves per interactive tool, as Halo's 7.1. The **Model half** is the
work between the input and a value the view can draw, timed by
`Tests/PrismModelTests/Prism<Feature>BudgetTests.swift` (median and p95 over
200 runs on the named fixture, on Linux and macOS; debug CI gates carry the
rubric's ×10). W0 adds the shared timing helper `PrismBudget.swift`. The
**UI half** is input to the first display refresh that shows it, read from
`SuiteFrameProbe` (on dev in `Sources/SuiteUI/FrameProbe.swift`) by the perf
journeys P-P1 to P-P5.

| id | Tool | What is timed | Fixture | Device target | CI gate | Test (feature) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PB-1 | Viewport redraw | `drawList` for one frame | `crowd` | ≤ 2 ms | ≤ 20 ms debug | `PrismViewportBudgetTests.drawListCrowd` (viewport) |
| PB-2 | Orbit, pan, dolly | one `orbit(dx:dy:)` plus `drawList` plus `overlay` | `crowd` | ≤ 3 ms | ≤ 30 ms debug | `.orbitStep` (viewport) |

A feature starts when everything it waits on has merged; it need not wait
for its whole wave. The lead playbook's stage 0 stops a lead whose
`Depends on:` issue is open, so each feature issue names one dependency: the
last of its waits to finish (Halo's filing rule).

## 10. Decisions and questions for the captain

### Calls this blueprint makes (overrule any of them)

| # | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Basic polygon modeling is tier 1 and in v1; subdivision and mirror are tier 2 and in v1 | Part 1.3: modeling is the most-done task (77.5%; 85% of motion-graphics users) and the top question topic |
| D2 | Render is tier 1 for Prism, unlike Halo and Flare | A 3D scene reaches the other apps only as a render (part 1.3) |
| D3 | Undo is whole-document snapshots, 100 steps, as Halo; a scene command engine in creative-core is later | Meshes are copy-on-write arrays, so a step copies only the mesh it edits; PB-8 and PB-9 bound it |
| D4 | RealityKit draws the viewport and renders finals offscreen; the Model owns the document and evaluates keys and modifiers; RealityKit animation is unused | One engine for viewport and render (what you see is what renders); SceneKit is deprecated; a Metal path tracer is tier 3 (4.1) |
| D5 | glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL and PLY in pure Swift in creative-core; the USD family through Model I/O; USDZ export in pure Swift | Nothing Apple ships reads glTF or writes USD on macOS 26; pure Swift is Linux-tested (4.1) |
| D6 | `.prism` is a package (`scene.json` plus binary buffers), not one JSON file | A 5M-triangle scene must open in seconds (PB-9b, P-J7) |
| D7 | The window runs on `.live` from W0 with every unbuilt command disabled and its reason as help; fixtures only by launch argument | No dead controls; no fake data in front of a person (Halo's D7) |
| D8 | Viewport single-key shortcuts act only with viewport or timeline focus | Plain-key menu equivalents would fire while typing (Halo's D5) |
| D9 | File > Render… is Prism's export of pixels and File > Export Model… its export of geometry | The domain's words; Render… sits where `docs/design.md` puts Export, as Halo's D9 |
| D10 | Prism uses `SuiteWindow`, Flare's floating palette and the switcher row, as the captain decided on 2026-10-15 | The look decisions |

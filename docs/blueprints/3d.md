# Prism blueprint (the AfterGlow 3D app)

Prism is the planned sixth app inside AfterGlow: open-source 3D tools for
scenes, models, materials, lighting, animation and rendering. You bring in a
model (or start from a primitive or extruded text and logo art), arrange it in
a scene, give it materials, light it, frame it with a camera, key it on a
timeline, and render stills or frame sequences that land in Halo, Flare and
Design. This blueprint is the gate before any code: it fixes the scope from
usage research, the components, every tier-1 journey, the use-case contracts,
the look, the quality bar and the build order. Feature packets
(`docs/blueprints/3d/features/`) come after the captain approves it.

Nothing exists for this app yet: no target, no Model, no engine, no issue.
Sources read on 2026-10-15: creative-apps `origin/dev` f2ef824 (`CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/design.md`, `docs/priorities.md`, `docs/engineering.md`,
`docs/c4/model.json`, `docs/factory/lead.md`, `docs/review-rubric.md`,
`docs/blueprints/app-audit.md`, `docs/blueprints/suite.md`, and the Flare and
Halo blueprints as the worked examples of shape and depth). The captain's look
decisions of 2026-10-15 apply: every app uses the `SuiteWindow` shell (glass
toolbar, sidebar, hideable inspector), Flare's floating glass tool palette,
and the app switcher row at the top of the window.

Files: this document; `docs/blueprints/3d/research.md` (raw research notes,
part 1); `docs/blueprints/3d/c4-planned.json` (part 4 as data);
`docs/blueprints/3d/contracts/` (part 6 as Swift that compiles on Linux, with
`check.sh`); `docs/blueprints/3d/waves.py` (part 9 as data, with its overlap

## 0. Working name

The suite's names are short, one word, and either a light word (Flare, Halo)
or a plain noun for the job (Design, Photo, Library). Three proposals; the
captain picks. This document uses the first throughout, and every id
(`PrismModel`, `prism.*` identifiers, `.prism` files, `line:prism`) is a
mechanical rename if another is picked.

| # | Name | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prism** | A light word like Flare and Halo, and the one everyday 3D solid that is about light: it reads as "3D and lighting" without saying "3D". |
| 2 | **Depth** | A plain noun for what the app adds to the suite, the third dimension, in the register of Design and Photo; short in a menu ("Open in Depth" reads less well, so it is second). |
| 3 | **Form** | The sculptor's word for shape in space; one syllable like Photo's two, and it leaves room for modeling (tier 2) without promising a full modeler. |

## 1. Research: what people use most in this kind of tool

Clean-room, as `docs/priorities.md` sets it: public sources only (open survey
data, public Q&A and forum counts, asset and add-on download counts, public
feature boards and issue trackers, help-center article lists). No 3D product
was installed, opened or run. Product names are replaced by the source key in
`docs/blueprints/3d/research.md`, where the raw notes are; a URL may still
contain a name. Gathered 2026-10-15.

**The strongest source** is the raw data of the most-used open-source 3D
suite's 2025 user survey (5,102 responses, CC BY 4.0), which asks "what do
you do" as a multi-select. It is self-selected and reaches only that suite's
users, so it over-counts modelers and generalists. It is still the only
public per-task usage measure we found, and its subgroups match our users:
1,273 people who do motion graphics, 1,973 who do graphic design, 1,769 who
call themselves designers. Every other row below agrees with its ranking.

### 1.2 What the open-source community is asking for now

- **Approachability for designers.** The biggest developer-forum threads are
  about the interface (4,020 posts on paper cuts; 1,409 on interface design),
  and an open letter (314 posts) says the suite "is rather complicated and has
  a steep learning curve" for designers who want to add 3D. People now build
  AI helpers just to point beginners at the right button
  (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45945890).
- **Interchange that works.** USD and USDZ support is the top format request
  on the most-starred web 3D library (73,
  https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/14219) and an open game engine
  (36, https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/7744); USDZ
  exports from the open-source suite fail in Apple's AR tools (missing
  textures, one animation only, https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/734220);
  the glTF exporter's most-commented issues are multiple animations (37) and
  speed (33).
- **Drag-and-drop import** of standard formats (210 + 136 votes) and **SVG
  to 3D** (designer-side forum, about 60 likes per request).
- **Direct, precise modeling**: base-point snapping (555), push/pull (510),
  align and distribute (153), editing the origin or pivot (71).
- **Real grouping** (364) and **undo everywhere** (a mesh-processing tool's
  top open issues are glTF 2.0 support and undo,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/441,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/376).
- **Fast on Apple silicon**, and a Mac-native feel (R18).
- **Texturing and animation tools** are the two most-wanted projects in the
  survey (42.2%, 33.3%); motion-graphics users say the open-source suite
  "takes many more clicks" than the commercial motion tool they know.

### 1.3 What the research decides

1. **The hypothesis holds for the loop and changes for modeling.** Import,
   arrange, materials, lights, cameras, keys and render are all in the top
   ten (R2, R4 to R6, R8, R10). But **basic polygon modeling is the single
   most-done task** (R1: 77.5% overall, 85% of motion-graphics people, 84.4%
   of graphic designers) and the top question and forum topic. So modeling
   is **tier 1 and in v1**, not tier 2 or 3: primitives plus an edit mode with
   extrude, inset, bevel, loop cut, merge, delete, fill and smooth shading.
   The subdivision and mirror modifiers that make box modeling usable are
   tier 2 and also in v1 (R7). Sculpting, procedural nodes, rigging and
   simulation stay out (R13 to R16).
2. **Render is tier 1, not tier 2** as it is for Halo and Flare: a 3D scene
   has no other way to reach the motion and design apps, and rendering is the
   third-largest question topic (R4).
3. **Interface basics are tier 1 work, not polish** (R3): the largest forum
   category and the largest idea category are about finding your way. That
   sets the look (part 7) and the first-use clarity bar (part 8).
4. **Environment light (an HDRI) ships with the first lighting feature** (R6:
   60% of free asset downloads).
5. **Text and vector-art extrusion is tier 2 in v1** (R9): it is the most
   common way designers start a 3D piece from Design and the suite's own
   hand-off in.
6. **FBX** is the most-asked format (820 titles) but it is a closed format;
   opening it is the captain's legal call (CLAUDE.md, Clean-room). v1 reads
   glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY and USDZ; part 10 asks.

## 2. Brief

### 2.1 The problem

Motion designers and graphic designers increasingly need 3D: a logo that
turns, a product shot, a title with depth, a scene behind a lower third. The
free tools that do this are built for generalist 3D artists. Their
interfaces are the most-discussed problem in their own communities (R3,
1.2), their Mac performance is a long-running thread (R18), and getting a
render into a compositor or a layout tool means exporting files and
importing them by hand. Prism is a Mac-native, free, open 3D app inside
AfterGlow that does the everyday 3D loop well: model or bring in an object,
place it, give it a material, light it, frame it with a camera, animate it,
and render it straight into Halo, Flare or Design.

### 2.2 Who uses it

| Person | Share of the research sample | Every session | Weekly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motion designer adding 3D** (Halo's and Flare's users) | 25.5% do motion graphics; of them 94% animate, 85% model, 67% light (R1, R2, R6) | Open or make a scene, model or import an object, key a move or turn, render a sequence to Halo | Extruded titles and logos, camera moves, environment light |
| **Graphic or product designer** (Design's and Photo's users) | 39.8% do graphic design, 32.6% product visualization; designers model (81%) and light (51%) (R1) | Model a simple product or prop, apply materials, light it, render a still to Design | Import a model from a marketplace (glTF, USDZ), export USDZ for AR |
| **Hobby generalist** learning 3D on a Mac | 52.9% use their tool daily; 50.3% earn nothing from it | Model, material, light, render | Animate, export to share |

### 2.3 Non-goals

- Sculpting with brushes, procedural node graphs, rigging and skinned
  characters, cloth, fluid, particle and rigid-body simulation (R13 to R16):
  each is a tool of its own.
- Compositing and video editing: Halo does that with Prism's renders.
- A production path tracer in v1. The v1 renderer is the viewport's own
  engine offscreen (part 4); a path tracer is a later tier-3 item.
- Game-engine features: physics at run time, scripting, level design.
- CAD: exact solids, dimensions and tolerances, technical drawings.
- UV unwrapping and texture painting in v1 (R12, R5): primitives come with
  UVs and imported models keep theirs.
- Reading or writing closed formats without the captain's sign-off (FBX, any
  vendor's native scene file); plugins; cloud or collaboration; iPad.

### 2.4 The v1 line

| Area | In v1 | Out of v1 (later, in order) |
|---|---|---|
| Scene and files | New scene (size, rate, length), open and save `.prism` packages, Open Recent through the suite list, undo and redo of every edit (100 steps), damaged and newer files refused with a message | Autosave and recovery (the suite policy, first Later item); linked (referenced) models |
| Viewport | Orbit, pan, dolly with mouse, trackpad and keys; frame selected; front, side, top and perspective views; grid and axes; solid and shaded modes; look through the active camera | Wireframe overlay mode; several viewports; local view |
| Objects | Add cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, plane, torus, an empty group, lights and cameras; select by click, box and outliner; move, rotate, scale by gizmo, inspector or keys; snapping to a grid; duplicate, delete, rename, hide, group, parent | Snap to vertices; align and distribute; arrays of objects |
| Modeling | Edit mode on any mesh: vertex, edge and face select (click, Shift-click, box, select all, edge loop); move, rotate, scale the selection; extrude, inset, bevel, loop cut, merge, delete, fill, flip, shade smooth or flat; Subdivision Surface and Mirror modifiers | Array, Boolean, Solidify and Bevel modifiers; knife, bridge; proportional editing; UV unwrapping |
| Text and vector art | Extrude a line of text (system fonts) or vector art from Design or an SVG file into a solid with depth and bevel | Text on a path; per-letter animation |
| Import and export | Import glTF/GLB, OBJ (with MTL), STL, PLY (pure Swift, Linux-tested) and USD/USDZ (macOS importer), by menu and drag and drop; export GLB, USDZ, OBJ, STL | FBX (needs the captain); Alembic; animation export in glTF |
| Materials | One PBR material model (base color or image, metallic, roughness, emissive); presets (Matte, Plastic, Rubber, Metal, Chrome, Gold, Glow); a material list per scene; assign by inspector or drag | Normal, roughness and metallic maps; clear coat; transmission (glass) |
| Lights | Directional, point, spot and area lights; environment color or image (HDRI), its strength and rotation; three lighting presets (Three-Point, Studio Soft, Rim); shadows | Light linking; IES profiles |
| Cameras | Add a camera, make one active, new camera from the view, field of view, look through | Depth of field, motion blur |
| Animation | Stopwatch and key-here per property (position, rotation, scale; light intensity; camera field of view); timeline lanes with key diamonds; scrub, play, loop at the scene rate; easing presets and a custom curve (SuiteUI `CurveEditor`, as Flare and Halo) | Graph editor; animating material values; follow a path |
| Render | Render the active camera (or the view) to a still PNG, a PNG sequence with alpha, a ProRes 4444 movie with alpha or an H.264 movie, at full or half size, with progress and Cancel | Path tracer; render passes; render queue |
| Hand-offs | Send a render to Halo (sequence or alpha movie), Flare (still or sequence) or Design (still), through the suite transport; paste or drop Design vector art to extrude; drop an image from Photo or Library as a texture or environment | Live link (re-render updates the placed footage); 3D layers in Halo |

**The line in one sentence:** a designer opens Prism, makes or imports an
object, shapes it with a few modeling tools, gives it a material and light,
keys a turn, and sends the render to Halo, without leaving AfterGlow.

# Prism blueprint (the AfterGlow 3D app)

Prism is the planned sixth app inside AfterGlow: open-source 3D tools for
scenes, models, materials, lighting, animation and rendering. You bring in a
model (or start from a primitive or extruded text and logo art), arrange it in
a scene, give it materials, light it, frame it with a camera, key it on a
timeline, and render stills or frame sequences that land in Halo, Flare and
Design. This blueprint is the gate before any code: it fixes the scope from
usage research, the components, every tier-1 journey, the use-case contracts,
the look, the quality bar and the build order. Feature packets
(`docs/blueprints/3d/features/`) come after the captain approves it.

Nothing exists for this app yet: no target, no Model, no engine, no issue.
Sources read on 2026-10-15: creative-apps `origin/dev` f2ef824 (`CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/design.md`, `docs/priorities.md`, `docs/engineering.md`,
`docs/c4/model.json`, `docs/factory/lead.md`, `docs/review-rubric.md`,
`docs/blueprints/app-audit.md`, `docs/blueprints/suite.md`, and the Flare and
Halo blueprints as the worked examples of shape and depth). The captain's look
decisions of 2026-10-15 apply: every app uses the `SuiteWindow` shell (glass
toolbar, sidebar, hideable inspector), Flare's floating glass tool palette,
and the app switcher row at the top of the window.

Files: this document; `docs/blueprints/3d/research.md` (raw research notes,
part 1); `docs/blueprints/3d/c4-planned.json` (part 4 as data);
`docs/blueprints/3d/contracts/` (part 6 as Swift that compiles on Linux, with
`check.sh`); `docs/blueprints/3d/waves.py` (part 9 as data, with its overlap
and C4 check).

## 0. Working name

The suite's names are short, one word, and either a light word (Flare, Halo)
or a plain noun for the job (Design, Photo, Library). Three proposals; the
captain picks. This document uses the first throughout, and every id
(`PrismModel`, `prism.*` identifiers, `.prism` files, `line:prism`) is a
mechanical rename if another is picked.

| # | Name | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prism** | A light word like Flare and Halo, and the one everyday 3D solid that is about light: it reads as "3D and lighting" without saying "3D". |
| 2 | **Depth** | A plain noun for what the app adds to the suite, the third dimension, in the register of Design and Photo; short in a menu ("Open in Depth" reads less well, so it is second). |
| 3 | **Form** | The sculptor's word for shape in space; one syllable like Photo's two, and it leaves room for modeling (tier 2) without promising a full modeler. |

Clean-room, as `docs/priorities.md` sets it: public sources only (open survey
data, public Q&A and forum counts, asset and add-on download counts, public
feature boards and issue trackers, help-center article lists). No 3D product
was installed, opened or run. Product names are replaced by the source key in
`docs/blueprints/3d/research.md`, where the raw notes are; a URL may still

**The strongest source** is the raw data of the most-used open-source 3D
suite's 2025 user survey (5,102 responses, CC BY 4.0), which asks "what do

### 1.2 What the open-source community is asking for now

- **Approachability for designers.** The biggest developer-forum threads are
  about the interface (4,020 posts on paper cuts; 1,409 on interface design),
  and an open letter (314 posts) says the suite "is rather complicated and has
  a steep learning curve" for designers who want to add 3D. People now build
  AI helpers just to point beginners at the right button
  (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45945890).
- **Interchange that works.** USD and USDZ support is the top format request
  on the most-starred web 3D library (73,
  https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/14219) and an open game engine
  (36, https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/7744); USDZ
  exports from the open-source suite fail in Apple's AR tools (missing
  textures, one animation only, https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/734220);
  the glTF exporter's most-commented issues are multiple animations (37) and
  speed (33).
- **Drag-and-drop import** of standard formats (210 + 136 votes) and **SVG
  to 3D** (designer-side forum, about 60 likes per request).
- **Direct, precise modeling**: base-point snapping (555), push/pull (510),
  align and distribute (153), editing the origin or pivot (71).
- **Real grouping** (364) and **undo everywhere** (a mesh-processing tool's
  top open issues are glTF 2.0 support and undo,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/441,
  https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/meshlab/issues/376).
- **Fast on Apple silicon**, and a Mac-native feel (R18).
- **Texturing and animation tools** are the two most-wanted projects in the
  survey (42.2%, 33.3%); motion-graphics users say the open-source suite
  "takes many more clicks" than the commercial motion tool they know.

### 1.3 What the research decides

1. **The hypothesis holds for the loop and changes for modeling.** Import,
   arrange, materials, lights, cameras, keys and render are all in the top
   ten (R2, R4 to R6, R8, R10). But **basic polygon modeling is the single
   most-done task** (R1: 77.5% overall, 85% of motion-graphics people, 84.4%
   of graphic designers) and the top question and forum topic. So modeling
   is **tier 1 and in v1**, not tier 2 or 3: primitives plus an edit mode with
   extrude, inset, bevel, loop cut, merge, delete, fill and smooth shading.
   The subdivision and mirror modifiers that make box modeling usable are
   tier 2 and also in v1 (R7). Sculpting, procedural nodes, rigging and
   simulation stay out (R13 to R16).
2. **Render is tier 1, not tier 2** as it is for Halo and Flare: a 3D scene
   has no other way to reach the motion and design apps, and rendering is the
   third-largest question topic (R4).
3. **Interface basics are tier 1 work, not polish** (R3): the largest forum
   category and the largest idea category are about finding your way. That
   sets the look (part 7) and the first-use clarity bar (part 8).
4. **Environment light (an HDRI) ships with the first lighting feature** (R6:
   60% of free asset downloads).
5. **Text and vector-art extrusion is tier 2 in v1** (R9): it is the most
   common way designers start a 3D piece from Design and the suite's own
   hand-off in.
6. **FBX** is the most-asked format (820 titles) but it is a closed format;
   opening it is the captain's legal call (CLAUDE.md, Clean-room). v1 reads
   glTF/GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY and USDZ; part 10 asks.

## 2. Brief

### 2.1 The problem

Motion designers and graphic designers increasingly need 3D: a logo that
turns, a product shot, a title with depth, a scene behind a lower third. The
free tools that do this are built for generalist 3D artists. Their
interfaces are the most-discussed problem in their own communities (R3,
1.2), their Mac performance is a long-running thread (R18), and getting a
render into a compositor or a layout tool means exporting files and
importing them by hand. Prism is a Mac-native, free, open 3D app inside
AfterGlow that does the everyday 3D loop well: model or bring in an object,
place it, give it a material, light it, frame it with a camera, animate it,
and render it straight into Halo, Flare or Design.

### 2.2 Who uses it

| Person | Share of the research sample | Every session | Weekly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motion designer adding 3D** (Halo's and Flare's users) | 25.5% do motion graphics; of them 94% animate, 85% model, 67% light (R1, R2, R6) | Open or make a scene, model or import an object, key a move or turn, render a sequence to Halo | Extruded titles and logos, camera moves, environment light |
| **Graphic or product designer** (Design's and Photo's users) | 39.8% do graphic design, 32.6% product visualization; designers model (81%) and light (51%) (R1) | Model a simple product or prop, apply materials, light it, render a still to Design | Import a model from a marketplace (glTF, USDZ), export USDZ for AR |
| **Hobby generalist** learning 3D on a Mac | 52.9% use their tool daily; 50.3% earn nothing from it | Model, material, light, render | Animate, export to share |

### 2.3 Non-goals

- Sculpting with brushes, procedural node graphs, rigging and skinned
  characters, cloth, fluid, particle and rigid-body simulation (R13 to R16):
  each is a tool of its own.
- Compositing and video editing: Halo does that with Prism's renders.
- A production path tracer in v1. The v1 renderer is the viewport's own
  engine offscreen (part 4); a path tracer is a later tier-3 item.
- Game-engine features: physics at run time, scripting, level design.
- CAD: exact solids, dimensions and tolerances, technical drawings.
- UV unwrapping and texture painting in v1 (R12, R5): primitives come with
  UVs and imported models keep theirs.
- Reading or writing closed formats without the captain's sign-off (FBX, any
  vendor's native scene file); plugins; cloud or collaboration; iPad.

### 2.4 The v1 line

| Area | In v1 | Out of v1 (later, in order) |
|---|---|---|

**The line in one sentence:** a designer opens Prism, makes or imports an
object, shapes it with a few modeling tools, gives it a material and light,
keys a turn, and sends the render to Halo, without leaving AfterGlow.

## 3. Feature catalog

Every feature, tiered from part 1 by the `docs/priorities.md` rule (tier 1:
nearly every session; tier 2: weekly; tier 3: specialist). Each entry says
what the person does, what they see, its empty, error and loading states,
its keys, and how it connects to the other AfterGlow apps. "Viewport keys"
act only while the viewport has focus (as Halo's Space and I/O act only in
its viewer and timeline), so typing in a field never moves anything; the
menu item shows the key in its help, not as a key equivalent. Every control
works or is disabled with its reason (`unavailableHelp`).

### Tier 1 (v1, the everyday loop)

**P-F1 New scene and scene settings** (R2, R4; the journey rule). File > New
Scene (⌘N) or the AfterGlow home's "New Scene" tile opens a window with a
starter scene: a ground grid, a 1-unit cube at the origin, one directional
light, the environment at a neutral grey, and a camera framing the cube at
1920 × 1080, 30 fps, 3 s. With no object selected, the inspector shows the
SCENE section: width, height, frame rate (24, 25, 30, 60), length in seconds
and frames, environment (part P-F13). *Empty:* File > New Empty Scene
(⌥⌘N) makes a scene with nothing but the grid; the viewport shows the
caption "Empty scene. Add a shape from the palette, or drop a model here."
with Add Cube as its one prominent button. *Error:* a typed size outside
16–8192 or a length of 0 stays unapplied, and the row shows "Width must be a
whole number from 16 to 8192." under it. *Loading:* none. *Keys:* ⌘N, ⌥⌘N.
*Connects:* the home's New tile and the switcher row's Prism item create
through the suite's `DocumentCreating` (suite.md UC3); presets "Square
1080", "Vertical 9:16" and "Landscape 1080p" match Halo's composition sizes
so a render drops into a Halo composition of the same size.

**P-F2 Open, save, Open Recent** (journey rule; R8's "drag and drop" asks).
File > Open… (⌘O), Save (⌘S), Save As… (⇧⌘S) and Open Recent. A scene is a
`.prism` package: `scene.json` plus one binary file per mesh and texture
(part 6, `PrismFilePackage`), so a 2-million-triangle scene opens without
parsing a giant JSON. The title shows the file name and an unsaved dot.
*Empty:* a new scene is "Untitled Scene" until saved. *Error:* a file that
isn't a scene, a newer format, a damaged package (a mesh that points at a
vertex it doesn't have, a missing buffer, two objects with one id, a parent
cycle) or one over 512 MB is refused with `PrismFileError.message` in an
alert, and the open window is untouched; a save to a read-only folder shows
"Not saved" in the title and an alert with the reason. *Loading:* opening
shows a progress row in the viewport within 100 ms (`beginOpen`), decodes
off the main actor, and lands with `finishOpen`. *Keys:* standard. *Connects:*
every open and save is recorded in the suite's one recents list, so the home
and the switcher's recents show it with a thumbnail (the last viewport
frame); Finder double-click on a `.prism` opens Prism through the suite
router (suite.md 2.6).

**P-F3 Undo and redo** (journey rule). Edit > Undo ⟨step⟩ (⌘Z) and Redo
(⇧⌘Z) for every scene and mesh edit, 100 steps, each named ("Undo Move",
"Undo Extrude", "Undo Change Material"). A gizmo drag, an inspector scrub
and a modeling-tool drag are each one step; selection, navigation, the
playhead and playback are never steps. *Empty:* Undo disabled with "Nothing
to undo." *Error:* none (an undo can't fail). *Loading:* none; HB-8 bounds
it. *Keys:* ⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z. *Connects:* a hand-off out or in (P-F18, P-F25) is one
step in the app that receives it, never in the sender.

**P-F4 Viewport navigation** (R3: the largest question and idea category).
Orbit with a two-finger trackpad scroll or Option-drag, pan with Shift added
or Space-drag (the suite's canvas rule), dolly with pinch or ⌘-scroll. The
viewport shows the scene shaded by its lights and environment, a ground grid
with the X axis red-tinted and Z blue-tinted, and an axis widget in the
top-right corner whose six ends click to the front, back, left, right, top
and bottom views (orthographic) and whose center returns to perspective. A
status pill row under the viewport reads the view ("Perspective", "Top ·
Orthographic", "Camera: Camera 1"), the triangle count and the frame.
*Empty:* the empty-scene caption of P-F1. *Error:* none: navigation clamps
(distance 0.01 to 100,000 units, pitch ±89.9°). *Loading:* a model still
importing shows its progress row over the viewport; navigation stays live.
*Keys (viewport):* F frame selected, A frame all (with nothing selected F
does the same), ⌥⌘1 front, ⌥⌘3 right, ⌥⌘7 top, ⌥⌘5 toggle orthographic,
⌥⌘0 look through the active camera; arrow keys orbit 15° (Shift: pan).
*Connects:* none out; the axis widget and the view pills are Prism's own
layout of SuiteUI parts (part 7).

**P-F5 Select** (R3, R11). Click an object in the viewport or its row in
the outliner to select it; Shift-click adds or removes; drag on empty space
draws a selection box; Select All (⌘A), Deselect All (⇧⌘A), Invert. The
selected object is drawn with an accent outline and its gizmo; the outliner
row is highlighted; the inspector shows its sections. *Empty:* clicking
empty space deselects and the inspector returns to SCENE. *Error:* none.
*Loading:* none. *Keys:* ⌘A, ⇧⌘A; Escape deselects in the viewport. 
*Connects:* none.

**P-F6 Outliner: organize the scene** (R11: a real grouping object, 364
votes). The sidebar's SCENE section lists every object as a tree with its
kind's SF Symbol (cube, lightbulb, video, folder). Rename by double-click,
drag to reorder or to drop into a group, the eye toggles visibility, Object >
Group (⌘G) wraps the selection in a group at their center, Ungroup (⇧⌘G),
Duplicate (⌘D, with its children), Delete (⌫ in the outliner or viewport).
Parenting keeps the object where it is on screen. *Empty:* "No objects yet.
Add one from the palette." *Error:* dropping a group into its own child is
refused: the row springs back and "An object can't be placed inside itself
or its children." shows under the outliner; an empty name keeps the old one
with "Enter a name." *Loading:* an import adds its rows when it lands.
*Keys:* ⌘G, ⇧⌘G, ⌘D, ⌫, H hide, ⌥H show all (viewport and outliner focus).
*Connects:* none.

**P-F7 Add primitives, groups, lights and cameras** (R1: the extra-primitives
add-on's 1M downloads). The palette's Add tool (and Object > Add ▸) offers
Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Plane, Torus, Empty Group, the four lights and
Camera. The new object lands at the center of the view on the ground (or at
the origin when the view looks below the horizon), selected, with the Move
gizmo on. *Empty:* not applicable. *Error:* none. *Loading:* none. *Keys:*
⇧⌘A is taken by Deselect All, so Add has no single shortcut; the palette's
Add tool is O ("object") with the canvas focused, which opens the menu.
*Connects:* none.

**P-F8 Transform: gizmo, inspector and snapping** (R1, R11). Move (W),
Rotate (E) and Scale (R) tools show a gizmo on the selection: three axis
arrows and three plane squares for move, three rings and a view ring for
rotate, three axis handles and a center cube for scale. Dragging a handle
moves live (a preview, not yet a step) and mouse-up commits one step. The
inspector's TRANSFORM rows show Position, Rotation and Scale X, Y, Z as
Studio bars (drag, click to type, arrows step, Shift ×10, Option ×0.1).
Holding ⌘ while dragging snaps (0.25 units, 15°, 0.1×); View > Snap to Grid
turns it on for every drag. *Empty:* with nothing selected the Move, Rotate
and Scale tools are enabled but show no gizmo, and the canvas hint reads
"Select an object to move it." *Error:* a typed "abc" keeps the value and
shows "Position X needs a number." under the row; a scale of 0 is kept at
0.0001. *Loading:* none. *Keys (viewport):* W, E, R, V (select); ⌘ snaps;
Escape during a drag cancels it. *Connects:* none.

**P-F9 Import models** (R8). File > Import… (⇧⌘I) or drop files on the
viewport: glTF and GLB, OBJ with its MTL, STL, PLY (pure Swift in
creative-core, so they are tested on Linux), USD, USDA, USDC and USDZ
(through the macOS importer, part 4). The import lands as one group named
after the file, selected and framed, with its materials in the material
list. *Empty:* a file with no meshes: "This file has no meshes in it."
*Error:* an unsupported extension names the formats Prism reads; a glTF or
OBJ whose buffers or MTL are missing names the missing file ("scene.bin is
missing. Put it next to the model and import again."); a damaged file says
what is wrong; over 5,000,000 triangles is refused before anything is added.
Skipped parts (skins, morph targets, extra UV sets, cameras in the file) are
listed in one dismissible note under the viewport, never an alert.
*Loading:* a progress row with the file name and Cancel within 100 ms; the
parse runs off the main actor; the viewport stays live. *Keys:* ⇧⌘I.
*Connects:* a model dragged from a suite asset (suite.md assets, tier 2
there) imports the same way.

**P-F10 Edit mode and element selection** (R1: edit-mode 2,946, vertices
2,489). Double-click a mesh or press Tab (viewport) to enter edit mode on it;
the rest of the scene dims, the mesh shows its vertices as dots, its edges
as lines and the selection in accent. The palette swaps to the modeling
tools (P-F11) and the inspector shows MESH (vertices, edges, faces,
triangles; the selection's center X, Y, Z as bars). 1, 2 and 3 switch
between vertex, edge and face selection; click, Shift-click, box drag, ⌘A,
and Option-click on an edge selects its loop. Tab or Escape leaves edit
mode. *Empty:* "Select vertices, edges or faces to edit them." as the canvas
hint while nothing is selected. *Error:* edit mode on a light, camera or
group is disabled with "Only objects with a shape can be edited." *Loading:*
none. *Keys (viewport):* Tab, 1, 2, 3, ⌘A, ⌥-click. *Connects:* none.

**P-F11 Modeling tools** (R1, R11's push/pull). In edit mode the palette
holds Select (V), Move (W), Rotate (E), Scale (R), Extrude (X), Inset (I),
Bevel (B) and Loop Cut (L), and Mesh menu items Merge at Center (⌥M), Fill
(⌥F), Delete (⌫), Flip Normals, Shade Smooth and Shade Flat. Extrude, Inset
and Bevel act by dragging in the viewport (the distance follows the pointer,
a value pill shows it, typing a number sets it exactly, Return commits);
Loop Cut previews the yellow-marked loop under the pointer and a click cuts
it (scroll before clicking sets 1 to 64 cuts). Each tool's result is one
named step and keeps the new elements selected. *Empty:* a tool with nothing
selected shows its hint ("Select faces to extrude.") and does nothing.
*Error:* a refused operation (extrude in vertex mode, merge of one vertex)
shows the reason under the viewport's status pills; a result that would
damage the mesh is refused whole. *Loading:* none; PB-6 bounds each step on
a 100,000-face mesh. *Keys:* as listed, viewport focus only. *Connects:*
none.

**P-F12 Materials** (R5). Each scene keeps a material list (the sidebar's
MATERIALS section, with a swatch per row). Selecting a mesh shows its
MATERIAL section: a picker of the scene's materials plus New Material, then
Base Color (`InspectorColorRow`, or an image), Metallic, Roughness and
Emissive Strength bars, Emissive color, Double Sided. The presets (Matte,
Plastic, Rubber, Metal, Chrome, Gold, Glow) are `ChoiceChips`; picking one
on a material sets its values in one step. Dragging a material row onto an
object in the viewport assigns it. *Empty:* a mesh with no material draws
in the default grey, and the section reads "Default material. Pick one or
make a new one." *Error:* a dropped image that can't be read: "Prism can't
read this image. Use PNG, JPEG or HEIC." *Loading:* a large texture decodes
off the main actor with a spinner on the swatch. *Keys:* none of its own.
*Connects:* drop or paste an image from Photo or Library as Base Color
(P-F25); copy a color from any suite app's color row and paste it on Base
Color (suite.md copy and paste, colors everywhere).

**P-F13 Lights and environment** (R6). Add a directional, point, spot or
area light (P-F7). Its LIGHT section: color, intensity (lux or candela, the
glTF punctual-light units), cone for spots, size for area lights, Shadows
on or off; the light shows a gizmo of its direction and cone. The scene's
ENVIRONMENT (in SCENE): a color or an equirectangular image (HDRI),
strength, rotation, and Transparent Background for renders. *Empty:* a scene
with no lights and a black environment renders black; the viewport caption
says "No light in the scene. Add a light or set the environment." *Error:*
an outer cone smaller than the inner one is refused with the reason. *Loading:*
a 4K HDRI decodes off the main actor; the viewport keeps the old light until
it lands. *Keys:* none. *Connects:* an environment image can come from Photo
or Library by drop or paste (P-F25).

**P-F14 Cameras** (R10). Add Camera, or View > New Camera from View (⌥⌘C)
to keep the current framing. CAMERA section: Field of View (bar, keyable),
Perspective or Orthographic, near and far clip, Make Active. The active
camera's frame (the output aspect) shows as a darkened passepartout when
looking through it (⌥⌘0). *Empty:* rendering a scene with no camera renders
the view and says so in the render sheet ("No camera: rendering the view.").
*Error:* far not beyond near is refused. *Loading:* none. *Keys:* ⌥⌘0,
⌥⌘C. *Connects:* the camera's size and rate are the scene's (P-F1), the
same numbers Halo's composition uses on the hand-off.

**P-F15 Keyframes, timeline and playback** (R2). Every keyable row in the
inspector (TRANSFORM's nine bars, light Intensity, camera Field of View)
has a `KeyframeButton`: the stopwatch starts animation (keys at the playhead
and the last frame), the diamond adds or removes a key at the playhead. The
timeline under the viewport has one lane per animated object with its key
diamonds, a playhead to scrub, and the transport (go to start, step, play,
step, go to end, loop). K keys the selection's position, rotation and scale
at the playhead in one step. Moving an animated object with the gizmo keys
it at the playhead; moving one that is not animated just moves it.
*Empty:* the timeline reads "Nothing animated yet. Click a stopwatch in the
inspector to start." *Error:* none (keys clamp like their rows). *Loading:*
none. *Keys:* Space play/pause, ← → step a frame, Home, End, K (viewport and
timeline focus). *Connects:* the timeline is the same SuiteUI `TimelineStrip`
and `TransportCapsule` as Halo and Flare, so it reads the same.

**P-F16 Easing presets** (R2; the same evidence as Flare and Halo). Pick a
key segment in the inspector's EASING group (rows like "Position Y 0 → 90")
and a chip: Linear, Ease In, Ease Out, Ease In and Out, Hold (the W3C CSS
easing presets). Animate > Keyframe Easing ▸ offers the same. *Empty:* "Key
a property to shape its easing." *Error:* the menu items are disabled with
"Pick a key in the EASING list first." until a segment is picked. *Loading:*
none. *Keys:* none of its own. *Connects:* the same presets and curve as
Halo, so a turn rendered from Prism eases like a Halo layer.

**P-F17 Render** (R4). Render Still (⌘R) renders the active camera at the
playhead into a preview pane over the viewport (Escape closes it) with
Save PNG… and Send To ▸. File > Render… (⇧⌘R, Prism's export, as Halo's)
opens a sheet: Still, PNG Sequence, Movie with Alpha (ProRes 4444) or Movie
(H.264); full or half size; frame range; Transparent Background; then
Render. Progress reads "Frame 48 of 90" with Cancel. *Empty:* not applicable.
*Error:* a size out of range, a frame range outside the scene, or a write
failure: the sheet names it ("Couldn't write the frame: the disk is full.")
and keeps what was written; Cancel leaves no partial movie. *Loading:* the
progress row within 100 ms; each frame renders off the main actor; the
window stays usable but edits are disabled until the render ends ("Rendering;
edits wait until it finishes."). *Keys:* ⌘R, ⇧⌘R, Escape cancels.
*Connects:* the finished sheet offers Send To ▸ (P-F18) and Reveal in
Finder.

### Tier 2 (weekly; in v1 where marked)

**P-F18 Send a render to Halo, Flare or Design** (v1; the brief's
"render out into Halo, Flare and Design"; R17: motion designers composite
renders). After a render, Send To ▸ Halo, Flare or Design (also File > Send
Render To ▸). Prism builds a `PrismHandOffRequest` (files, rate, size,
alpha, the source `.prism`) and the suite transport opens it in the target:
Halo places the sequence or alpha movie as a footage layer in a new or the
front composition of the same size and rate; Flare places a still or
sequence as an image layer; Design places a still as an image. *Empty:*
Send To is disabled before a render with "Render first, then send it."
*Error:* a format the target can't take names the right one ("Design can't
take this kind of render. Pick Still."); a target whose image import has
not shipped is disabled with that app's reason from the suite registry.
*Loading:* the suite's switch progress (suite.md SJ2). *Keys:* none.
*Connects:* needs image or footage placement in Halo (Halo's "image and
footage layers" is Later in halo.md), Flare and Design: asks for those
lines, part 9.

**P-F19 Extrude text** (v1; R9). Object > Add ▸ Text, or the palette's Text
tool (T): type a line; TEXT section: string, font (system font picker),
size, Depth, Bevel width, Bevel segments. The text is a mesh rebuilt from
glyph outlines (Core Text on the Mac, through `GlyphOutlining`) by
creative-core's extruder, so it keys, materials and renders like any mesh;
Object > Convert to Mesh makes it editable in edit mode. *Empty:* an empty
string is refused with "Enter some text." and the old text comes back.
*Error:* a font with no outlines for a character skips it and says which.
*Loading:* none (≤ 16 ms per rebuild, PB-9). *Keys:* T. *Connects:* text
typed in Design can be pasted as text.

**P-F20 Extrude vector art** (v1; R9's SVG asks). Paste vector art copied
in Design (the suite pasteboard's SVG representation), drop an SVG file, or
File > Import… an SVG: its filled shapes become one extruded mesh per shape,
grouped, with each fill color as a material. VECTOR section: Depth, Bevel,
Segments. *Empty:* art with no closed filled shapes: "There's no closed
shape to extrude." *Error:* SVG features outside SVG 1.1 paths, basic shapes
and solid fills (gradients, filters, text) are skipped and listed in the
note. *Loading:* progress row for large files. *Keys:* ⌘V. *Connects:*
Design → Prism, the suite's vector hand-off in (suite.md copy and paste:
vector art Design → Flare uses the same SVG representation).

**P-F21 Subdivision Surface and Mirror modifiers** (v1; R7). The selected
mesh's MODIFIERS section: Add ▸ Subdivision Surface (levels 1 to 3 in the
viewport, 0 to 4 in renders) or Mirror (X, Y, Z, merge at the seam). Each
modifier is a row with an eye and a delete; edit mode edits the cage and
shows the result live. *Empty:* "No modifiers. Add one to smooth or mirror
this shape." *Error:* a level that would exceed 5,000,000 triangles is
capped with the reason. *Loading:* none; PB-7. *Keys:* ⌥⌘S adds
Subdivision. *Connects:* export bakes modifiers into the mesh.

**P-F22 Export a model** (v1; R8, 1.2's USDZ asks). File > Export Model…
(⇧⌘E): GLB (geometry, materials, textures), USDZ (geometry and materials,
for Apple's AR viewer), OBJ and STL (geometry, for printing). Keys are baked
at the playhead. *Empty:* an empty scene: Export disabled with "Add an
object to export." *Error:* a write failure names the reason. *Loading:*
progress row. *Keys:* ⇧⌘E. *Connects:* none (files leave the suite here).

**P-F23 Lighting presets** (v1; R6). Scene > Light the Scene ▸ Three-Point,
Studio Soft or Rim adds a named group of lights around the selection (or
everything) in one step. *Empty:* in an empty scene the items are disabled
with "Add an object to light first." *Error:* none. *Loading:* none. *Keys:*
none. *Connects:* none.

**P-F24 Custom easing curve** (v1; the curve editor is tier 2 for Flare).
The EASING group's `CurveEditor`: drag the two handles, type the four
numbers; "Custom" then shows on the segment. Same states as P-F16.

**P-F25 Images in: textures and environment** (v1; R5, R6). Drop or paste an
image (from Photo, Library or the Finder) on an object to make it that
object's Base Color image, or on the ENVIRONMENT row to light the scene with
it. *Empty, error, loading:* as P-F12's image row. *Connects:* Photo and
Library → Prism, through the suite pasteboard's image representations.

**P-F26 Array, Boolean, Solidify and Bevel modifiers** (later, v1.1; R7).
**P-F27 UV unwrapping and a checker view** (later; R12). **P-F28 Snap to
vertices, align and distribute, set origin** (later; R11). **P-F29 Depth of
field and motion blur** (later; R10). **P-F30 Graph editor** (later; R2).
**P-F31 Autosave and recovery** (later; the suite policy, first Later item).
Each gets its full entry in its packet when it is scheduled; none has a v1
journey.
